## Session Goal

Session 14 is intentionally a lower-stakes adventure following the emotional climax of the Water Temple ritual. The session should introduce the heroes to the Savage Lands as they truly are, begin Leon's personal story arc, and establish the path toward the campaign's final conflict without immediately escalating into another major climax.

The session's primary combat encounter is intended to occur relatively early (roughly mid-session), allowing the latter half to focus on mystery, worldbuilding, and player-driven exploration.

---

# Opening Encounter - The Herd

The heroes are traveling with members of the Savage Spire.

The guides suddenly stop.

Not because they are frightened.

Because they recognize something.

One quietly says:

> "A herd."

There is no panic.

The Savage folk immediately begin positioning themselves between the approaching animals and the heroes while calmly discussing unrelated matters.

This should immediately communicate that:

- This is dangerous.
- This is common.
- These people know exactly what they're doing.

The heroes quickly realize these are **Primal animals**.

Not corrupted.

Not diseased.

Simply far more powerful than ordinary wildlife.

Examples:
- A Primal bear possesses the instincts and strength of several bears.
- A Primal hyena possesses multiple times the aggression, territorial instinct, and endurance of a normal hyena.

Primal creatures are naturally volatile because their instincts are amplified.

Most medium and large Primals possess aggressive responses simply because aggression exists somewhere within their natural instincts.

This is simply how nature works inside the Savage Lands.

The Savage people bond with Primals partly because surviving alongside them requires understanding them.

---

# Combat Tone

The Savage Spire warriors are experienced professionals.

They do not shout orders.

They do not panic.

They simply advance, defend one another, and converse almost casually while fighting.

This should reinforce that these warriors have survived countless encounters like this.

Their calmness should impress the heroes far more than flashy combat.

---

# Casual Dialogue During Combat

While engaging the herd, snippets of conversation naturally occur.

Examples:

> "Three herds this week."

> "Five."

> "The Elder won't like that."

---

> "Has anyone checked on the tree-man?"

> "Yesterday."

> "Still rooted?"

> "Still rooted."

---

> "Still asking?"

> "Every day."

---

> "Asking what?"

> "...whether we've found any others from inside."

Silence.

Then combat simply continues.

No explanation.

No one elaborates.

The line should feel almost forgotten by the speakers.

To the heroes, however, it should immediately raise questions.

---

# Player Agency

After the herd is defeated, the Savage warriors continue their pursuit deeper into the continent.

They explain that unusually large herd movements have become more common.

Whether the heroes accompany them is entirely their decision.

The intended hook is curiosity rather than obligation.

Possible motivations:

- The mention of the mysterious "tree-man."
- The Elder's interest in him.
- Questions about "others from inside."
- The increasing frequency of herd migrations.
- The source of the Primal energy.

Nothing forces the heroes onward.

The mystery should naturally invite investigation.

---

# Pierre (Leon's Father)

Pierre successfully passed through the first two layers of the Fool's Curtain by coating himself in Voidcap mushroom paste.

The final layer consists of oppressive magical malice.

It destroys anything without sufficient will.

Plants perish almost instantly.

Animals survive only briefly.

Creatures with greater determination survive longer.

Pierre's determination allowed him to nearly escape.

Just before leaving the final layer, one stray thought entered his mind:

Leon.

The thought broke his absolute resolve.

The malice immediately began destroying him.

Although he escaped the Curtain alive, he suffered catastrophic consequences:

- aged approximately forty to fifty years
- soul infused with malignant magical energy
- magical "perforations" formed throughout his body
- the malicious energy continually tore outward through him

The effect resembles magical buckshot constantly exiting from within.

Survival itself became miraculous.

---

# Arrival at Savage Spire

A salvage vessel discovered Pierre barely alive and brought him to the nearest settlement:

Savage Spire.

The Mycelial Elder believed Pierre's only chance of survival was forming a Primal Bond.

However...

Pierre was too weak.

Even the smallest nearby Primals required strength beyond what remained of him.

A hunting expedition therefore traveled deeper into the Savage Lands seeking stronger Primal energy.

---

# The Gold Dragon

Deep within the Savage Lands lies an ancient Gold Dragon.

The dragon has been magically wounded.

The wound was inflicted by Malazar.

Rather than healing...

The wound continually leaks raw Primal energy into the surrounding wilderness.

This leakage explains:

- the extraordinary abundance of Primals
- increasingly powerful wildlife
- Primal plants
- the overwhelming magical vitality near the dragon

---

# Pierre's Bond

When Pierre reached the dragon...

His magical perforations reacted directly against the dragon's open wound.

Instead of requiring a formal bonding ritual...

The bond occurred naturally.

Pierre bonded with a nearby **Baobab Tree Primal**.

The transformation stabilized him.

He survived.

However...

He became rooted.

Unable to return.

Because trees grow slowly...

Pierre retained much of his original appearance.

Observable characteristics include:

- exposed roots
- leaves emerging from his body
- rough Baobab bark forming over portions of his skin
- aged appearance
- otherwise recognizable facial features

A successful Perception check should make these details increasingly obvious.

---

# Pierre's Personality

Pierre never stopped being a researcher.

Even rooted.

Even transformed.

He continues documenting observations.

Asking questions.

Developing theories.

Trying to solve the mysteries before him.

Rather than asking for rescue...

He asks questions.

He records information.

He studies.

His greatest contribution to the campaign is not combat.

It is understanding.

---

# The Gold Dragon's Wound

Pierre eventually reaches a critical conclusion.

The dragon is **not failing to heal.**

The dragon is healing constantly.

Something simply prevents the wound from ever closing.

Analogy:

The wound is like a knife left buried in flesh.

Every attempt to heal merely forms around the obstruction.

As long as the knife remains...

The wound continually reopens.

---

# Malazar's Influence

Malazar created the wound.

Only Malazar's own magical signature can safely interact with it.

Ironically...

The heroes now possess fragments of that same signature.

By sharing Killian's curse...

They unknowingly inherited tiny portions of Malazar's own corruption.

These fragments become the only means of interacting with the magical "knife."

However...

Doing so requires further attunement to the curse.

The heroes must willingly embrace additional corruption.

Only then can they grasp the magical obstruction and remove it.

---

# Why Pierre Cannot Heal It

Pierre understands the problem.

He understands the solution.

He lacks the necessary connection to Malazar.

He possesses knowledge.

The heroes possess the required resonance.

Only together can the wound finally be healed.

---

# The Primal Leak

Once the obstruction is removed...

The dragon immediately attempts to heal naturally.

However...

The wound has remained open for so long that it lacks sufficient unbound Primal energy to begin sealing itself.

It needs a catalyst.

A living "seed."

---

# The Final Sacrifice

The Savage Spire people provide the answer.

Voluntarily.

Those accompanying the heroes willingly surrender their own Primal Bonds.

Their bonded Primal energies return to the dragon.

These become the initial "seed patch" from which the wound finally closes.

The participants survive.

They simply become fully human once again.

To the Savage people, this is not viewed as losing power.

It is returning a sacred gift to its rightful source.

The heroes sacrifice by embracing greater corruption.

The Savage people sacrifice by surrendering the gifts that have defined their lives.

Together...

Both sacrifices finally allow the Gold Dragon to heal.

With the Primal leak ended:

- no new uncontrolled Primals are created
- the Savage Lands begin returning to balance
- future generations need not bear the same burden
- Malazar's greatest wound upon the land finally begins to mend
